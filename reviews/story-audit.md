# Story audit: All the Terrible Things We Didn't Think

Reviewed the complete `game.story`, the eight included scripts in their declared order, `VOICE.md`, `ASSETS.md`, and spec sections 2.4 to 2.8. Source references below are relative to `examples/terrible-things/`.

Read-only engine checks: `graph` shows a shared main story followed by three endings. `explore` reports 42 routes, 103,123 states, 28/28 scenes and 537/537 lines covered, including a second playthrough for the true-ending unlock. These are the explorer's coverage figures, not a count of every possible choice sequence. Replayed a route that skips the question hub into Fourteenth, a route with all questions and supportive choices into Salt, and a low-bond route into Kindest after Fourteenth. No game, engine or test files were changed. No git commands were run.

The strongest problems are conflicting rules for eligibility and thought transfer, two references to conversations that may never happen, and personal goals left open in the shorter endings. The missing summer has a substantive payoff on every ending. The prose and sensitive subject matter need a focused pass, while the Countess sequence and much of the character-specific humor already work.

## 2. Continuity and logic

### Rules and knowledge

1. **Keeper eligibility changes at the moment it is needed.** Suzu defines clear as “someone who's never given a bead, or it won't take” (`story/ch2.story:59`). Nagi later says “My only bead came home last night. I'm clear again” (`story/ch6.story:94`), then successfully takes the Heart (`story/endings.story:16` through `story/endings.story:22`). Returning a bead does not undo `never`. Recommended rule: clear means no thought of yours is currently stored outside you. State it during the tour, and keep the lottery definition consistent (`story/ch6.story:36`). Most regular visitors still have older beads stored, but explain why returning a willing adult's remaining beads is not a usual alternative to choosing children.

2. **Consent, proximity and shared transfer need separate rules.** “you can't give anyone a thought they won't hold” (`story/endings.story:136`) sounds universal. In S, the four people nearest the Heart receive its contents without choosing to (`story/endings.story:90`, `story/endings.story:91`). Living owners also receive broken beads automatically. Define deliberate transfer separately from what a shattered container does. Establish shared holding before Suzu proposes it at `story/endings.story:137`. S's “if everyone had been there” (`story/endings.story:87`) and the replay hints (`story/ch6.story:95` through `story/ch6.story:98`) provide some preparation, but F alone also unlocks T. The physical solution should be intelligible through that sequence too.

3. **Optional conversations are remembered unconditionally.** “I promised you the Glasshouse” (`story/ch2.story:37`) refers to the beads question (`story/ch1.story:142`). “You asked why I asked for you by name. I told you I picked it out of a hat” (`story/ch4.story:29`) refers to question four (`story/ch1.story:152` through `story/ch1.story:157`). Both lines run after skipping the hub entirely. Make the tour a fresh invitation and the revelation a fresh admission, or track the specific questions. The total `asked` cannot identify which were chosen.

4. **The lottery narrator claims knowledge the route may not provide.** “I know exactly which thought just went through her. I know now. One fewer” (`story/ch6.story:49`) is Nagi's assertion about Hinata. On the silent kitchen branch, Hinata has told her only “Nothing much. Bad dreams” (`story/ch5.story:59`). The reader saw Hinata's POV; Nagi did not. Even on the confession branch, knowing an earlier thought does not establish this exact new one. Mark this as Nagi's inference or let Hinata disclose it afterward.

5. **The past thought changes from inference to a factual cause.** The memory says Suzu squeezed Nagi's hand “as if she'd heard” (`story/ch4.story:54`); T says she stepped forward “because she heard me” (`story/endings.story:108`). No intervening confirmation supplies that knowledge. Establish what Suzu perceived and why she chose, or preserve uncertainty. This also affects the theme, discussed below.

6. **Suzu's bodily damage needs a consistent final inventory.** At the garden, “Only her face is still her own” (`story/ch6.story:78`), after the heart becomes visible through glass ribs. F says the glass “stopped where it was: her hands, her eyes, her throat” (`story/endings.story:31`). Include the affected arms and torso or state what recovered. Do not diagnose the earlier wrist/collarbone descriptions as definite regression: `ASSETS.md:40` explicitly establishes a separate glass collar from the start. The interlude's collarbone description (`story/interlude.story:19`) and the later “In one night” milestone (`story/ch4.story:26`) nevertheless make the progression harder to picture.

7. **A few causal links are compressed rather than contradicted.** The answer to “Why now?” (`story/ch4.story:83`, `story/ch4.story:84`) uses tonight's broken shelves and tomorrow's lottery to explain a summons made before the festival. Her already approaching death is a plausible earlier motive; link the two times explicitly. Likewise, T's statues breaking with Suzu (`story/endings.story:178`) needs a small earlier indication that they remain linked to the system. The new clear fingertips (`story/endings.story:181`) plausibly represent a finite shared cost; saying the change has stopped would remove an avoidable fear of another fatal progression.

8. **Extraction's scope is elastic.** Removing Nagi's wish removes the entire person associated with it (`story/ch4.story:62`). Hinata repeatedly removes thoughts about losing her whole family while retaining those relationships (`story/ch5.story:17`, `story/ch5.story:18`). This is not a proven contradiction, since the thoughts differ, but the exceptional memory loss drives the whole mystery. Give the reader one specific reason Suzu's identity was inseparable from this extraction, while other memories survive.

No firm name, age or nine-year/ten-summer contradiction was found. Nine completed summers and the start of the tenth are explicitly distinguished (`story/interlude.story:19`). The apparent empty house before Suzu's parents move (`story/ch4.story:63`, `story/ch4.story:64`) is presented inside Nagi's altered memory, so it should not be treated as a straightforward relocation error.
